This script builds a 1D numerical ocean model that simulates how heat diffuses vertically down through the ocean column over time. We use Finite Difference Method to solve the 1D Heat Diffusion Equation from scratch. 
Firstly we define the spatial grid (dz=10.0, nz=50) and time parameters (dt=3600, Nt=2000), then we initialize an exponential thermocline profile starting at 25°C at the surface and decaying to 5°C at depth.
We apply the 1D heat diffusion equation (T_new[k] = T[k] + K * d2T_dz2 * dt) inside a time loop and set a seasonal sine wave at the surface boundary (T_new[0]) while holding the deep boundary fixed at 5°C (T_new[-1]).
We use plt.plot to compare the initial profile ('k--') against the model result (teal) after 83 days of simulated diffusion.
Finally we lock the depth axis between 500 meters to 0 meters with (plt.ylim(500, 0)) and invert the Y-axis (invert_yaxis()) to observe how surface heat diffuses downward into the ocean interior.
Vertical mixing engines like these form the core computational 3D climate supercomputer models like MITgcm.
